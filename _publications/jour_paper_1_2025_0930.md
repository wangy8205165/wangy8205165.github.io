Online learning is a machine-learning paradigm in which models are updated continuously as new data arrive, rather than trained once on a fixed dataset. By processing data sequentially, online algorithms adapt in real time and are naturally suited to large data streams and changing distributions (concept drift). Their ability to work under tight memory and latency constraints makes them valuable in areas such as personalized recommendation, financial forecasting, and fraud detection.

This review surveys core online learning algorithms—covering supervised, unsupervised, and related approaches—within a clear taxonomy. It offers a critical look at how these methods perform in real-world settings, with a special focus on fraud detection, and highlights the gap between theoretical advances and practical deployment. The article concludes with promising research directions for narrowing that gap and strengthening the impact of online learning in operational environments.
